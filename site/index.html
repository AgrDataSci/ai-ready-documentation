Share one link. Get the evidence, protocols, data and analysis behind the research.

Agricultural research leaves papers, protocols, data and code in separate places, and using them takes someone who already knows where to look. maniva links and documents them, so anyone, and any AI assistant, can use them without that insider.

tricot is the first domain, with on-farm trial data on more than 30 crops in more than 25 countries since 2011. Climate and household data come next.

Four layers, one entry point

Each layer lives in its own repository, with its own version, licence and DOI. maniva routes each question to the layer that answers it. Pick a question to see the route.

Questions people ask

  1. Knowledge base

    What does the published evidence say?

    Verified cards on the open-access tricot literature. Findings carry their primary citation and the people behind them.

    • tricot-knowledge-base

    Active, assistant guide ready

  2. Protocols

    How do I run a trial?

    Standard operating procedures, surveys and trait metadata in five languages, with traits mapped to the Crop Ontology.

    • tricot-protocols

    Available, assistant guide planned

  3. Data

    What did the trials find?

    Harmonized trial data in JSON, one file per trial, archived on Zenodo under CC0.

    • tricot-data

    Available, assistant guide planned

  4. Analysis

    How do we reason with the data?

    Curated use cases with decision records on methods and their limits, the R packages they run on, and a template for reproducible, FAIR projects.

    • tricot-analysis-workflows
    • gosset
    • ClimMobTools
    • climatrends
    • chirps
    • ClimMob-analysis
    • template-repo-data-analysis

    Active, assistant guide ready, packages on CRAN

The layers connect through shared identifiers. A paper card names the dataset its findings rest on, a dataset names the protocol that produced it, and protocols and data share trait definitions from the Crop Ontology.

How we keep it trustworthy

  • One repository per layer. Each stays the single source of truth and keeps its own citation and DOI.
  • Verified content. Numbers are checked against their sources and carry their primary citation. Uncertainty is stated, not smoothed over.
  • History is never rewritten. Corrections are new commits, logged in the changelog and checkable against an independent archive. See the provenance policy.
  • Open by default. Data under CC0 1.0, software under MIT, documentation, protocols and methods under CC BY 4.0.

Why maniva

Cassava is propagated from the maniva, a stem cutting that carries the potential for new growth. The crop was domesticated in the Amazon and travelled to Africa and Asia, adapting to new environments on the way.

maniva is meant to work the same way, so that knowledge developed in one place can take root, adapt and create value elsewhere.

Rooted in biodiversity. Growing through collaboration.

ma‑NEE‑va

From Old Tupi mani'yba, the stem of the manioc plant. Cassava is also one of the crops in the trial data.

Cite maniva

When you use a specific layer, cite that repository too, and cite the original papers for any finding.

de Sousa K, Laporte MA, Youngberg B, Cremaschi A, Chase R, van Etten J (2026). maniva. An open commons of evidence, protocols, data and analysis for agricultural research. Version 1.0.0. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24011051
